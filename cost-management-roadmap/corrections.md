Mastering Cost Management - corrected roadmap

What this is

- A revision of the cost management roadmap infographic. Three sheets, tabloid landscape (11 x 17 in), printable.
- Sheet 1: lifecycle foundations, the language of cost, and the money measures.
- Sheet 2: fit-out cost boundaries, lab and infrastructure scenarios, and the monthly control cycle.
- Sheet 3: every correction made to the previous version.

How it was checked

- Every element of the previous version was transcribed and reviewed, one reviewer per panel.
- Each finding was then attacked twice and independently: once on technical accuracy, once on industry practice.
- A finding was dropped only when both attacks succeeded.
- 97 errors raised, 9 dropped, 88 carried into the corrected sheets. 20 of those are critical.

Every figure on the sheets is illustrative, chosen so the arithmetic ties. None of it is project data.


Corrections


1. Lifecycle foundations

1. Was: The PDCA ring read ACT – DO – ACT – DOO. PLAN and CHECK were missing.
   Now: PLAN, DO, CHECK, ACT — each step once, clockwise, with ACT closing back into PLAN.  [critical]
2. Was: Both circles carried the same heading, "THE TCM FRAMEWORK".
   Now: One heading. The two circles are the framework's two process maps, captioned separately.
3. Was: The project control circle had no cycle steps at all.
   Now: Drawn as the same four-step loop, one level down.
4. Was: The two circles sat unconnected, reading as two independent systems.
   Now: Linked: plans go in at DO, results come back at CHECK. Project control is recursive inside asset management.  [critical]
5. Was: "(Business-red)".
   Now: "(business / enterprise level)", paired with "(project / technical level)".
6. Was: IDEATION › PLANNING › EXECUTION › EXECUTION › CLOSURE.
   Now: Four stages, EXECUTION shown once.  [critical]
7. Was: Four bars rising to CLOSURE, captioned "cost influence greatest" at planning.
   Now: Two curves: influence falling 100→0, cumulative spend rising 0→100, crossing near 62% of duration. The old chart said the opposite of its own caption.  [critical]
8. Was: "The project delivery path", used as a heading twice.
   Now: "The project life cycle", used once. Delivery method is a separate decision.
9. Was: The asset life cycle drawn as a one-way run to termination.
   Now: A return arc: operation and modification repeat many times before termination.

2. The language of cost

10. Was: Reproduction and replacement cost captioned as the decision "whether to move existing equipment or buy new".
   Now: They are valuation bases, not a decision. Keep-or-replace is a separate defender-and-challenger test on future cash flows.  [critical]
11. Was: "ECONOMIC COST TERMS" printed twice, with the numbering restarted at 1.
   Now: One heading, one list, numbered 1 to 7 without a restart.
12. Was: SUNK COST appeared twice, the second time verbatim.
   Now: Listed once.
13. Was: "INVESTINY CRIST (Esprect Processon)".
   Now: Investment (first) cost — capital to acquire, install and commission, ready for use.
14. Was: Book cost defined as "accounting value".
   Now: A non-cash cost carried in the accounts. Book value is a different thing: first cost − accumulated depreciation.  [critical]
15. Was: Cash cost absent, so book cost stood alone.
   Now: Added. Book cost is recorded now; the cash left earlier.
16. Was: "Budgets define the term".
   Now: "Perspective defines the term" — budgets do not decide which cost term applies.
17. Was: "Parogone Benefit" and "Imwurenside Past Spend".
   Now: "The benefit of the best alternative given up" and "unrecoverable past outlay".
18. Was: Neither reproduction nor replacement was said to be priced at current costs.
   Now: Both stated as priced at current costs, at the estimate date.

3. Measures, variance, forecast

19. Was: A funnel placed FORECAST FINAL COST last and narrowest.
   Now: The EAC is drawn as its own bar and is allowed to cross the budget line — which is the alarm, not a drawing error.  [critical]
20. Was: Six items presented as one family of "money measures".
   Now: One baseline, four ledger states, one forecast. They are not the same kind of number.  [critical]
21. Was: Incurred → invoiced → paid stated as a universal law.
   Now: True for progress-billed work. Advances and milestone terms pay before cost is incurred.
22. Was: No cut-off date anywhere.
   Now: Every measure reported at one stated cut-off date, accruals cut the same day.
23. Was: Budget, forecast and headroom drawn as three icons with no operator.
   Now: Budget − EAC = variance at completion. Worked: 100.0 − 103.5 = -3.5.  [critical]
24. Was: Only the favourable case was drawn.
   Now: Both signs. Positive is headroom; negative is a forecast overrun with a named action.
25. Was: Nothing warned that headroom must be taken against the forecast.
   Now: Budget − paid and budget − committed are budget remaining, and ignore the cost to complete.
26. Was: The two build columns were drawn at different heights, captioned "both must agree".
   Now: Identical lengths, with the tie-out printed: 32.5 = (84.0 − 71.0) + 12.0 + 4.5 + 3.0.  [critical]
27. Was: The forecast bridge was a drawing of a suspension bridge.
   Now: A waterfall: opening anchor, six signed movements that sum exactly, closing anchor. No balancing bar.  [critical]
28. Was: "SCHPE CHANRE" and "Duth must agree".
   Now: "Scope change" and "both must agree".

4. Fit-out boundaries

29. Was: Direct cost defined as a cost "built for one objective".
   Now: Traced to one cost object. Dedication is not the test — traceability is.  [critical]
30. Was: Direct and indirect presented as fixed properties of a cost.
   Now: The classification depends on which cost object is named. Supervision is indirect to the lab chamber, direct to the project.  [critical]
31. Was: Indirect cost stopped at "supports several objectives".
   Now: Indirect is recovered by allocation on a declared driver: rate = pool ÷ driver quantity.
32. Was: Monthly crane hire labelled FIXED, with "per month" given as the definition of fixed.
   Now: Three classes: fixed, time-related, variable. Only the time-related slice grows in a delay.  [critical]
33. Was: "Direct does not always mean variable" — half the statement.
   Now: Both directions, drawn as a 2 × 3 grid with every cell occupied.
34. Was: A two-way scope split: core and shell against tenant improvement.
   Now: Three bands. The middle tier — landlord fit-out to lettable standard, UK Cat A / US warm shell — was missing.  [critical]
35. Was: The interface note was unreadable.
   Now: Set one demarcation point for every IT, AV and security system, carried on a signed scope matrix.
36. Was: The panel named one classification and carried three.
   Now: The heading names all three: traceability, behaviour, scope.

5. Lab and infrastructure scenarios

37. Was: "Excludes MEP, IT racks, specialised equipment" was hung on the LAB side.
   Now: Backwards. The exclusion belongs to the office benchmark; the lab rate is the number that includes them.  [critical]
38. Was: A head-to-head with a figure on one side only.
   Now: Both bars carry a total and a composition on one common zero-based scale, plus the delta between them.  [critical]
39. Was: COST B = COST A × (Cap B + Cap A).
   Now: COST B = COST A × (Cap B ÷ Cap A)n. The capacities divide.  [critical]
40. Was: The exponent had dropped out, surviving as a stray quote mark. No value of n anywhere.
   Now: n restored as an exponent. n ≈ 0.6 for plants; range 0.5–0.9, rising toward 1.0 at large scale.  [critical]
41. Was: An equals sign between a 90-unit plant and a 138-unit plant.
   Now: +53% capacity for +29% capital, and -16% per ton per day. The two plants are not equal.  [critical]
42. Was: "90 ten-per-day thermsl plant", and the second label silently dropped "thermal".
   Now: Tons per day, thermal, on both labels. Same plant type both sides or the ratio means nothing.
43. Was: No validity limits on the rule.
   Now: Valid roughly 0.5× to 2× the base capacity, same technology. Escalate and location-adjust first. Screening level only.
44. Was: Staged scored on lifecycle cost, ultimate on net present value.
   Now: One metric for both: NPV of life-cycle cost, one discount rate, one horizon, one basis.  [critical]
45. Was: "Building ultimate capacity now is often cheaper", printed as the standing answer.
   Now: Conditional. Ultimate wins only when escalation plus amortised remobilisation beats the discount rate and demand is firm. Crossover here: 7.5% real.  [critical]
46. Was: No discount rate, horizon, escalation rate or demand assumption stated.
   Now: All four printed with the chart, and flagged illustrative.


Challenged, and left as it was

1. The balance scale for price against cost.
   A level beam under a "versus" heading reads as "compare these two", not as "these are equal". Kept.
2. "Cost influence is greatest at planning".
   Standard front-end-loading teaching. The error was the chart underneath it, not the statement. Kept.
3. "Standardised metrics to prevent counting things twice".
   Double counting is exactly the failure common cost definitions exist to prevent. Kept.
4. "Incremental cost: the future difference between alternatives".
   Usage is not uniform across the standard texts, and this reading is one of the accepted ones. Kept.
5. "TI" as the name of the occupier scope tier.
   In US practice TI names the work; the landlord's contribution is the TI allowance. Kept, with the UK term alongside.
6. "Capacity factor" as the name of the method.
   AACE RP 59R-10 calls it capacity factored estimating. Kept.
7. The building cutaway and the plant icons.
   Pointing devices, not claims. Kept, with the mechanism drawn over the top.
